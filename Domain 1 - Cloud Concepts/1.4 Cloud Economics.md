# 1.4 Understand concepts of cloud economics

> [!abstract] Exam guide objective (verbatim from the CLF-C02 exam guide)
> **Knowledge of:**
> - Aspects of cloud economics
> - Cost savings of moving to the cloud
>
> **Skills in:**
> - Understanding the role of fixed costs compared with variable costs
> - Understanding costs that are associated with on-premises environments
> - Understanding the differences between licensing strategies (for example, Bring Your Own License [BYOL] model compared with included licenses)
> - Understanding the concept of rightsizing
> - Identifying benefits of automation (for example, provisioning and configuration management with AWS CloudFormation)
> - Identifying managed AWS services (for example, Amazon RDS, Amazon Elastic Container Service [Amazon ECS], Amazon Elastic Kubernetes Service [Amazon EKS], Amazon DynamoDB)

## Big picture

Cloud economics is about why running on AWS usually costs less overall than running your own data center, and which choices keep it that way. The exam tests vocabulary (CapEx vs OpEx, fixed vs variable cost, TCO), the hidden costs of on-premises IT, the two licensing models (BYOL vs license included), rightsizing, the value of automation with AWS CloudFormation, and what "managed service" means. This note is conceptual; the specific pricing models (On-Demand, Reserved Instances, Savings Plans, Spot) and billing tools are in Domain 4: [[4.1 AWS Pricing Models]] and [[4.2 Billing, Budget, and Cost Management]].

## Key concepts

### CapEx vs OpEx, fixed vs variable costs

| Term | Definition | On-premises example | AWS example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Capital expenditure (CapEx)** | Money spent **up front** on physical assets you own, depreciated over years | Buying servers, storage arrays, a building | Rare; a 3-year All Upfront Reserved Instance is the closest analogue |
| **Operational expenditure (OpEx)** | Ongoing spending on services and day-to-day operations | Electricity, staff salaries, leases | Monthly AWS bill |
| **Fixed cost** | Stays the same no matter how much you use | A server you bought costs the same whether it is 5% or 95% busy | Commitment-based pricing (a Reserved Instance or Savings Plan) is a fixed commitment |
| **Variable cost** | Rises and falls with usage | Little; most on-premises costs are fixed | On-Demand compute, S3 storage per GB, Lambda per request |

The cloud's core economic shift is ==**trading fixed (capital) expense for variable (operational) expense**==: you stop paying up front for capacity you may not use and pay only for what you consume. Benefits: no large upfront investment, no stranded capacity, costs that track business activity, and lower risk when trying new ideas (if an experiment fails, you stop paying).

### Costs associated with on-premises environments

On-premises total cost goes far beyond the server price. Questions often ask which costs **disappear or shrink** when moving to AWS.

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Hardware** | Servers, storage, network equipment (switches, routers, load balancers, firewalls), spare parts, hardware refresh every 3 to 5 years |
| **Facilities** | Data center space or colocation rent, power, cooling, physical security, fire suppression, cabling, racks |
| **Software** | Operating system, database, and virtualization licenses; support and maintenance contracts |
| **Labor** | Staff to rack, stack, power, patch, back up, and repair; procurement and capacity planning time |
| **Over-provisioning** | Capacity bought for peak demand that sits idle most of the time |
| **Indirect / opportunity** | Long procurement lead times, slower time to market, disaster recovery site duplication |

### Total cost of ownership (TCO)

**Total cost of ownership (TCO)** is the full cost of a solution over its life, including the direct costs (hardware, software) and indirect costs (facilities, power, cooling, labor, downtime). A fair cloud vs on-premises comparison compares **TCO**, not just server price vs instance price. AWS usually lowers TCO through economies of scale, no idle capacity, and less operational labor. Tools: **AWS Pricing Calculator** estimates the cost of an AWS architecture ([[Services - Cost Management#AWS Pricing Calculator|AWS Pricing Calculator]]); **Migration Evaluator** builds a data-driven business case for migrating. (Older study material mentions an "AWS TCO Calculator"; the estimating tool named in the CLF-C02 exam guide is AWS Pricing Calculator.)

### Licensing strategies: BYOL vs license included

| Model | What it means | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **License included** | The software license cost is built into the AWS hourly price (for example, Amazon EC2 Windows instances, Amazon RDS for SQL Server or Oracle "License Included"). AWS handles license compliance for that software. | You have no existing licenses, want simplicity, or need short-term or variable usage. Pay as you go. |
| **Bring Your Own License (BYOL)** | You reuse licenses you already own (subject to the vendor's terms) on AWS, and pay AWS only for infrastructure. Some vendor terms require **Dedicated Hosts** (a physical server dedicated to you) for socket-, core-, or VM-based licensing. | You have already paid for licenses (often with Software Assurance or similar) and want to avoid paying twice. |

**AWS License Manager** helps you manage software licenses from vendors such as Microsoft, SAP, Oracle, and IBM centrally across AWS and on-premises environments: you define licensing rules, track usage, set hard or soft limits, and reduce the risk of non-compliance and audit penalties. See [[Services - Management and Governance#AWS License Manager|AWS License Manager]].

### Rightsizing

**Rightsizing** means matching instance types and sizes to the actual workload performance and capacity requirements at the **lowest possible cost**. You look at utilization data (CPU, memory, network) and downsize or change family for over-provisioned resources, and turn off idle ones. It is an ongoing process, not a one-time task, and is usually the first cost optimization step **before** buying Reserved Instances or Savings Plans (otherwise you commit to the wrong size).

Tools that recommend rightsizing:
- **AWS Compute Optimizer**: uses machine learning on utilization metrics to recommend optimal EC2 instances, Auto Scaling groups, EBS volumes, Lambda functions, and more ([[Services - Management and Governance#AWS Compute Optimizer|AWS Compute Optimizer]]).
- **AWS Cost Explorer** rightsizing recommendations ([[Services - Cost Management#AWS Cost Explorer|AWS Cost Explorer]]).
- **AWS Trusted Advisor** cost optimization checks, such as low-utilization EC2 instances and idle load balancers ([[Services - Management and Governance#AWS Trusted Advisor|AWS Trusted Advisor]]).

### Benefits of automation (AWS CloudFormation)

**Infrastructure as code (IaC)** means defining infrastructure in text files (templates) instead of clicking in the console. **AWS CloudFormation** lets you model, provision, and manage AWS resources from a JSON or YAML **template**; the set of resources it creates is a **stack**. CloudFormation itself is free; you pay for the resources it creates.

Benefits the exam looks for:
- **Repeatability and consistency**: deploy identical environments (dev, test, prod) or the same stack in many Regions and accounts.
- **Speed**: provision a full environment in minutes, which supports agility.
- **Fewer human errors**: no manual click-by-click steps.
- **Version control and auditability**: templates live in source control, so changes are reviewed and tracked.
- **Easy cleanup and rollback**: delete the stack to remove every resource; failed updates roll back automatically.
- **Lower cost**: tear down environments when not needed and recreate them on demand.
- **Configuration management**: keep resources in a known, documented configuration (AWS Systems Manager handles configuration and patching **inside** instances).

### Managed services

A **managed service** is one where AWS operates the underlying infrastructure and handles undifferentiated tasks such as hardware provisioning, OS and software patching, backups, high availability setup, and scaling, so you focus on your application and data. Managed services shift more of the [[2.1 Shared Responsibility Model|shared responsibility model]] to AWS and reduce operational labor, which is a major source of TCO savings.

| Service | What AWS manages for you | What you still manage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Services - Database#Amazon RDS\|Amazon RDS]] | Hardware, database software installation and patching, OS patching, automated backups, Multi-AZ failover, storage scaling options | Schema, queries, data, access (security groups, IAM, database users), choosing instance size |
| [[Services - Database#Amazon DynamoDB\|Amazon DynamoDB]] | Fully managed, serverless NoSQL: no servers, no patching, automatic replication across multiple AZs, scaling | Table design, data, access policies |
| [[Services - Containers#Amazon Elastic Container Service (Amazon ECS)\|Amazon ECS]] | The container orchestration control plane (scheduling, placement, scaling of tasks) | Your containers and task definitions; the EC2 instances too, unless you use AWS Fargate |
| [[Services - Containers#Amazon Elastic Kubernetes Service (Amazon EKS)\|Amazon EKS]] | The Kubernetes control plane (availability, patching, scaling) | Your Kubernetes workloads; worker nodes unless you use Fargate or managed node groups |

Compare with running the same software yourself on Amazon EC2: you would patch the OS, install and upgrade the database or orchestrator, configure replication and backups, and handle failover.

### Other cost savings of moving to the cloud

- **Pay-as-you-go** and **no upfront commitment** for most services.
- **Economies of scale** and periodic AWS price reductions ([[1.1 Benefits of the AWS Cloud]]).
- **Elasticity**: no idle capacity bought for peak.
- **Purchasing options** that reward commitment or flexibility: Reserved Instances, Savings Plans, Spot Instances ([[EC2 Purchasing Options]]).
- **Volume discounts** (tiered pricing, consolidated billing in AWS Organizations).
- **Reduced labor** through managed services and automation.

## Exam traps and commonly confused

> [!warning] CapEx vs OpEx
> On-premises hardware purchases are **CapEx**. The AWS bill is **OpEx**. "Moving to AWS converts capital expense to variable/operational expense" is a correct statement; "AWS eliminates all operational expense" is wrong.

> [!warning] Rightsizing vs Reserved Instances
> Rightsizing changes **what size or type** you run. Reserved Instances and Savings Plans change **how you pay** for it. Rightsize first, then commit.

> [!warning] Rightsizing vs elasticity
> Rightsizing picks the correct size for steady needs. Elasticity (Auto Scaling) changes the **number** of resources as demand changes. Both save money.

> [!warning] BYOL and Dedicated Hosts
> If a question says existing licenses are tied to **physical cores or sockets** and must be reused, the answer usually involves **Dedicated Hosts**, not Dedicated Instances. See [[EC2 Purchasing Options]].

> [!warning] License Manager vs AWS Marketplace
> **License Manager** tracks and enforces licenses you have. **AWS Marketplace** is a catalog to **buy** third-party software (often with license included).

> [!warning] CloudFormation vs Systems Manager vs Elastic Beanstalk
> **CloudFormation** provisions infrastructure from templates (IaC). **Systems Manager** operates and patches what is already running. **Elastic Beanstalk** deploys an application and provisions the platform for you without writing templates.

> [!warning] Managed does not mean "no responsibility"
> With RDS, AWS patches the database engine and OS, but you still manage your data, access control, and network settings. With EC2 you patch the guest OS yourself.

## Quick self-check

> [!question]- Is buying servers for an on-premises data center a fixed or variable cost?
> Fixed (a capital expense paid up front regardless of usage).

> [!question]- Name four on-premises costs that are reduced or removed by moving to AWS.
> For example: hardware purchase and refresh, data center space, power and cooling, physical security, and staff time for racking, patching, and maintenance.

> [!question]- What is rightsizing?
> Matching instance types and sizes to actual workload needs at the lowest cost, based on utilization data.

> [!question]- Which service gives machine-learning-based recommendations for the optimal EC2 instance types?
> AWS Compute Optimizer.

> [!question]- A company already owns Microsoft SQL Server licenses and wants to use them on AWS. Which licensing model is this?
> Bring Your Own License (BYOL).

> [!question]- Which service helps track and enforce software license usage across AWS and on premises?
> AWS License Manager.

> [!question]- Name three benefits of provisioning infrastructure with AWS CloudFormation.
> Repeatable and consistent deployments, fewer manual errors, speed, version control, and easy teardown or rollback.

> [!question]- Which of these is a managed AWS service: Amazon EC2 or Amazon RDS?
> Amazon RDS. (EC2 is infrastructure you manage.)

> [!question]- What does TCO include beyond the price of servers?
> Facilities (space, power, cooling), software licenses, labor, maintenance, network, and over-provisioned capacity.

> [!question]- With Amazon ECS on AWS Fargate, who manages the servers that run the containers?
> AWS.
